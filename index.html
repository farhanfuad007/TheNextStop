Why this matters

The most expensive mistake in a student's life
is rarely the exam. It's the guess.

The problem

Every year, thousands of students sit for admission tests having spent months preparing for exams — and almost no time understanding the actual subjects they're applying for. They pick a department based on a rumor, a relative's opinion, or a ranking list, not because they understood what four years inside that subject actually looks like.

The regret

Somewhere between the first semester and graduation, a huge number of students quietly realize they're in the wrong place. Not because they failed — but because nobody showed them, before they enrolled, what a chemical engineer actually does on a Tuesday, or that "Computer Science" and "Software Engineering" are not the same degree at all.

The fix

You cannot undo a wrong decision made blind. But you can prevent one. Explore every category, every subject, every sub-field — the real admission path, the real day-to-day, and the honest regrets other students report — before you write a single subject choice on a form.
